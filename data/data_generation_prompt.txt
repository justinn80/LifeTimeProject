PACE - Data Generation Prompt (Phase 1)
======================================

Data strategy: synthetic data, generated with an LLM plus Python (as stated in
the approved proposal, Section 5).

LLM used:  Claude (Anthropic), November 2026
Output:    src/generate_data.py  (the simulator the LLM wrote to this spec)
           data/staffing_scenarios.csv  (100 staffing plans, the analysis dataset)
           data/hourly_demand.csv       (952 simulated center-hours behind it)

Why a generator instead of pasting a CSV from the chat:
An LLM that types 100 rows of numbers by hand cannot keep cost, staffing hours
and service levels arithmetically consistent with each other, and the result
cannot be reproduced. So the prompt asks the LLM for a seeded Python simulator
built to an explicit spec. Every number in the dataset can then be traced back
to a stated assumption, and anyone can regenerate it with
`python src/generate_data.py`.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXACT PROMPT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

I'm building PACE (Predictive Allocation for Club Excellence), a
multi-objective staffing optimizer for one representative Life Time
(NYSE: LTH) athletic country club. I need a synthetic dataset of 100 weekly
staffing plans for Pareto frontier analysis. Write a seeded, reproducible
Python script (numpy + pandas only) that simulates the club and scores each
plan.

DECISION VARIABLES (a full 10 x 10 grid = 100 plans)
1. peak_coverage_pct: staffing in peak windows as a % of the demand-based
   staffing target. Range 60 to 150 in steps of 10. Peak windows are weekday
   5-9 AM and 4-8 PM, weekend 8 AM-12 PM.
2. offpeak_coverage_pct: staffing in all other open hours as a % of the same
   target. Range 40 to 130 in steps of 10.
   Coverage applies to all three flexible labor groups: fitness-floor staff
   (attendants and on-shift trainers), group fitness instructors (number of
   classes offered), and childcare attendants.

OBJECTIVES
1. labor_cost_per_visit (MINIMIZE): weekly flexible labor cost (floor +
   instructors + childcare, including payroll burden and overtime) divided by
   completed member visits.
2. member_experience_score (MAXIMIZE, 0-100): weighted composite of
   - class fill-fit (35%): classes should be full but not overbooked. Score 1
     when a class is 75-95% full, falling to 0 for near-empty rooms and to 0.9
     for sold-out rooms, multiplied by the share of class demand that got a
     spot.
   - floor time-to-assistance (40%): model floor help requests as an M/M/c
     (Erlang C) queue per hour, add walk time, and map 1 minute -> 100 and
     10+ minutes -> 0.
   - childcare availability (25%): share of children requesting care who are
     admitted.
   Weight every hour by the number of members who experienced it, so peaks
   matter more than empty midday hours.

DEMAND (randomness so no two weeks, and no two days, are the same)
- Calibrate weekly check-ins to Life Time's Q1 2026 results: 837,903 center
  memberships across 190 centers (about 4,410 per center), with an assumed 3.0
  check-ins per membership per week.
- Weekday hourly curve with a pre-work rush (~6:30 AM), a lunch bump and a
  larger after-work rush (~5:30 PM). Weekends get one broad late-morning peak.
  Sunday is quietest.
- Add week-level, day-level and hour-level noise. Simulate 8 weeks and use
  COMMON RANDOM NUMBERS: every plan is evaluated on the same 8 weeks, so
  differences between plans come from the staffing decision, not from luck.

HARD RULES (every generated plan must obey these)
- Childcare ratio of 1 attendant per 6 children. Children beyond capacity are
  turned away, never squeezed in. Two-adult minimum while the kids area is
  open.
- At least 2 floor staff on duty every open hour.
- 3 studios at 35 members each (room capacity caps concurrent classes).
- Overtime at 1.5x past each role's straight-time roster hours.
- Staff are scheduled in half-hour blocks.

REALISTIC PATTERNS I EXPECT
- Under-staffing peaks should hurt experience badly (queues blow up, classes
  sell out, childcare turns families away, and a turned-away parent often
  skips the workout).
- Over-staffing should cost more and help less. Too many classes leave rooms
  half empty, so the class fill-fit score falls. That should create genuinely
  dominated plans.
- Off-peak staff should be cheap per hour but buy little experience, since few
  members are in the building.

OUTPUT
- data/staffing_scenarios.csv with columns: plan_id, peak_coverage_pct,
  offpeak_coverage_pct, labor_cost_per_visit, member_experience_score, plus
  supporting columns (weekly_labor_cost, weekly_visits, staff hours by role,
  classes_per_week, total_labor_hours, overtime_hours, the three component
  scores, average and peak time-to-assistance, class fill rate, class waitlist,
  childcare turnaways, and the week-to-week standard deviation of both
  objectives).
- data/hourly_demand.csv with the simulated hour-level demand.
- Put every assumption in one PARAMS dictionary with comments, and add a
  --check flag that regenerates the data and asserts it matches the saved CSV.
